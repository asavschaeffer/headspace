TheBausFFS Playstyle

Renowned League of Legends player Simon "TheBausFFS" Hofverberg rose to fame largely due to his easygoing personality and unorthodox playstyle. In a game as cutthroat as League, where the smallest misstep could cause a teammate to quit, The Baus is intentionally "inting" or dying, in order to gain himself an advantage in the long run. If his teammates can withstand the crucible of the early-mid game defeat, The Baus will surely put the team on his massive undead back before the fat lady sings.

Even for those who have never played League, or never really even gamed, it doesn't take a game theorist to explain that intentionally dying over and over isn't a winning strategy. In Multiplayer Online Battle Arena (MOBA) games, as opposed to First Person Shooter (FPS) games, the swing of balance of power due to game events is more swift. Players continuously kill "minions" or "creeps", which are small, largely powerless units, each kill earning a small amount of gold. Killing another player grants a large amount of gold, and puts the other player on the bench for a period of time. This is a double-win: the killer gets bonus gold, continues to "farm" the minions, while the die-er gets no bonus gold and loses out on the minions. In Call of Duty, in even the harshest, most competitive gamemodes, such as Search and Destroy, dying only removes you from the action. It doesn't reward your enemy in any way more than that. It doesn't make their gun shoot faster, increase their movement speed or durability, at the most it would trigger some specific perks in the killer to briefly increase their health regeneration and afford them some ammunition to scavenge.

This is the essential game mechanic of the MOBA that makes them so intense. Every kill/death matters. If you can kill your opponent early in the game, you have the potential to start a little snowball with that lead, push the advantage so that your opponent has less opportunity to farm creeps, grow the snowball bigger and bigger and create such a strong advantage over your opponent that you can extend it across to all members of your team and take over the game. League players, for the entire history of the game, have had this burned into their minds.

Then comes The Baus For Fucks Sake, who utilizes a unique champion passive in order to secure the gold reward for the creeps after death. He puts himself in a compromising position and takes their time and attention away from farming creeps to chase him around. The result in the end is that Baus farms the creeps and gets the reward, and the opposing player has to choose to farm their creeps and ignore the Baus, or to kill the Baus and leave the creeps and the gold they have to offer. Usually players decide to kill him, it is second nature now, kills are so important and the creeps are never ending. And the Baus dies, having pushed the wave of minions so far back that they will take a long time to return to their normal position, equidistant from both team's safe point. At this point, the rewards for the exchange are +300 for the opponent, +150 for Baus. As it happens over and over, the opponent gets marked as a high value target by the game, granting increased bounty gold to anybody who can kill him. The player's safe haven is gradually plundered by Baus as he continually pushes the minions further and further up at the cost of his life. Finally, the value of killing Baus drops lower and lower, as the game calculates that he is not a valuable target. At this point, the rewards for the exchange are +150 for the opponent, +150 for Baus. Now, all he needs is to claim that bounty for a massive upset.

And he usually does. League is like baseball, it's hard to go without making any mistakes. And while the Baus is dying, it's not like he is making mistakes and is unskilled. He is extremely skilled, mechanically and strategically, in controlling his character to the fullest of its potential. He knows every potential game state, which ways to spend his income to gain advantage over different opponents, when they are at a vulnerable point, and how to exploit every opportunity. So, yeah, eventually, he will get the bounty gold. And at that point, the rewards for the exchange are -150 for the opponent, and +500~1000 for the Baus.

There are many other technicalities and mechanics at work to make this playstyle shine. The character that the Baus plays is essential. He does it on other characters, but they all have certain qualities that make the strategy possible. His main character, Sion, has:

  1. a passive that allows him to gain a few seconds of furious battle, after death, to kill minions or chase around other players. This passive also cuts into the total time waiting "on the bench" after death.
  2. unique champion passive that rewards a small amount of max health per minion kill.
  3. massive scaling, damage, wave clear, and crowd control potential as the game goes later.

So Simon "The Baus FFS" has taken advantage of the frustrating bounty system, which punishes players for getting a lead, and created a winning strategy. It's okay to die, if you can get something from it too. While he is rewarding his opponent and making them stronger, he is keeping himself in the game, and setting himself up for a greater reward in the future. It's like an investment. You choose the right time to die, that causes massive distraction and chaos in the ranks of the enemy, and then you return later to take the crown from the king and place it in the mess of shattered bone where your undead head can hold it. Truly, he plays Sion the way the Loremaster intended.
