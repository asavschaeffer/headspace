Vermintide 2 Fire Mage Analysis

Date: 2025-03-25 | Status: Draft | Project: N/A | 70% Complete

Introduction

I purchased Vermintide 2 a long time ago on Steam, probably on discount, and let it steam in my library for a year or so without ever playing it. My friend told me to buy it so we could play together one day. He told me it’s essentially fighting off hordes of rat people, so of course I was in. The bonding of friendship through trials of battle touches the deepest roots of our ancestral heritage as humans against the rodents, serpents, and predators.

So when I saw a long-time-no-see friend started playing Vermintide 2, I immediately plugged in my ethernet cord and downloaded it as fast as possible. I played through the prison break tutorial, I was impressed with the graphics and the level design and vibe. When it came to choose my character though, I was stumped.

Character Selection Dilemma

The leveling system is based on individual characters, so while you might be able to share items between characters, the passive skill trees and career choices are only unlocked by playing an individual character. It’s easy to switch, but for me it still meant that I’ve gotta choose once, and choose well. Each character has some ranged and melee options, a few different playstyles, but their own solid identity:

  • Big tank guy wielding a 2h weapon and a 3 musketeers hat.
  • Dwarf with a crossbow.
  • Elf assassin with a bow, knives, or 2h sword.
  • Rapier guy.
  • Fire wizard.

In my gaming career, I’m generally more successful on a tankier playstyle. In SMITE my highest winrate was Geb or Athena, in League of Legends, Malphite and Aatrox. When I played Destiny 2 I was dominant as a Titan but lost as a Warlock. In Elder Scrolls Online I played every class, but I probably remember playing Skaffa’s iconic double sword and shield builds. I just am a crayon-eating tank player and I enjoy the role of crowd-control, supporting and buffing allies, and generally taking control and responsibility for the dungeon situation.

So as a tank player I thought I would play dwarf or the big guy with the feather in his cap. The problem was that my friend also likes to play tank, and coincidentally I was immediately attracted to the mad fire mage during the tutorial. Which leads me to my true RPG calling. I love magical knights. And Vermintide 2 features a melee-focused career for the fire witch named Fuegonasus.

That decided it for me. I’ve played maybe 2 hours now, and I’m close to unlocking level 11 for Fuegonasus and I’ll be able to test the “Unshackled” fire warrior then.

Concerns: Unshackled Career

The Unshackled, thematically, is the fire mage who has fully succumbed to the addictive, thrilling power that comes with the wind of Aqbar. As a result, the build is properly themed around the explosion and the maximum heat. But it is improperly implemented. Her ability is to detonate the overheat charge, dissipating it back to basic. Thematically, this would mean that she has fully mastered the wind, and has perfect control of it, able to let it rise and dissipate it at will. Thematically though, that is not the case for her. She is the opposite of that, she has lost her control of the flame and is a servant of it.

Proposed Changes

I propose a change. The Unshackled should be the second career, not the third. Her ability will be the Firewalk that currently is being used by the Battle Wizard class. It doesn’t make sense for the most melee-focused career to be the least mobile. The Battle Wizard can have a protective fire rune like the one used in the Necromancer DLC cutscene. The new third career, which was the single-target focused version, without mobility, can stay largely the same. If she wants to represent the opposite of Unshackled, she can have an overheat offloading bonus when activating her ability at high levels.

Now to the Unshackled. If we want to thematically show an addict to the fire, we can do two main things: utilize the explosion on death from overheat, and promote that death as a viable playstyle. This would create a unique, dynamic, thrilling, risky, and most importantly, legitimate playstyle. The passive ability could be to restore her to life, or heal her based on enemies hit with the fire death overheat explosion, but I think that would be too easy. She should die, but definitely she should kill everything around her. Then her teammates could res her without much trouble. Her passive can stay the same, receiving overheat bar when receiving damage. This could scale with missing health, ensuring that she is more likely to overheat when on low health, which is reasonable to expect from a fire-crazed rogue wizard.
