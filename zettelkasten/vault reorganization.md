> [!tip]
> Start with the basics and a few notes. Understand the tool. Check it with real data. Don't overcomplicate things.
> 
> If you start with plugins and very complex things, you'll get lost quickly. If you have a good understanding of the tool and its limitations, you'll get to understand when plugins are really required and how to better use them.
> 
> Understand that your vault is a living thing and it will change a lot with time. It should change with time, as you do change and the questions you ask today aren't the same ones you'll be asking in 5 years from now.
> 
> Start with the official documentation, replicate examples with your notes. Once you've gone through it, then add more notes. When you have some dozens or maybe hundreds of notes, and if you can't answer a question with your notes, then start looking for a plugin to solve that specific need. Repeat the process.
> 
> Plan your notes for the questions you have today. Plan them to answer time based questions, for example, by adding the required metadata to your notes. Plan categories, tag usage: what are they used for in your vault, what are the conventions you use (initial in caps all in lowercase? always plural or always singular? what will be a category and what will be a tag in your vault?) and so on.
> 
> You'll only get a good output if you plan and dedicate time for good input.
> 
> But don't get stuck. As I said, your vault is a living thing and you can start simple and evolve it from there. It is better to have bad input than no input at all...
