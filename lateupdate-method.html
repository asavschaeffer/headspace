LateUpdate Method

[Ref: Unity Learn Lesson 1.3.5]

BUG: In Unity, when a camera follows a moving object, stuttering can occur if the camera and object's movements are not synchronized. This stuttering is often caused by inconsistencies in the update timing of the camera and object scripts.

Why it Occurs

  • Update() Method: The Update() method is called every frame, and it's the primary method for updating game objects and their behaviors.
  • Timing Inconsistencies: Due to variations in frame rates and other factors, the camera and object might not always be updated at the exact same time. This can lead to discrepancies in their positions, resulting in stuttering.

What is Update() and LateUpdate()?

  • Update(): A built-in Unity method that is called every frame. It's the primary method for updating game objects and their behaviors.
  • LateUpdate(): Another built-in Unity method that is also called every frame, but it executes after all Update() methods have finished.

How is it Fixed?

By using LateUpdate() for the camera script, we ensure that the camera's position is updated after the object's position. This helps to synchronize their movements and eliminate the stuttering effect.

  1. Locate the Camera Script: Find the script attached to your camera GameObject.
  2. Replace Update() with LateUpdate(): Change the Update() method in the camera script to LateUpdate().
  3. Save and Test: Save the script and test your game to see if the stuttering is resolved.

Additional Considerations

  • Frame Rate: While LateUpdate() can help mitigate stuttering, maintaining a consistent frame rate is also important for smooth gameplay.
  • Other Factors: Other factors like complex calculations or heavy rendering can also contribute to stuttering. Optimizing your game code and ensuring efficient resource usage can help improve performance.
