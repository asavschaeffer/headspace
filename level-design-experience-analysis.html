II. Freewrite

Chinese-Tibet mountain roadway

I have only a little bit of level design experience from when I made a Minecraft PvP minigame early in high school. I used command blocks and Bukkit commands to teleport players to different pressure plates that would give them items, and after a brief delay teleport them to their starting positions in the arena. That battleground was a take on sky islands, I guess you could say, and I included a central island with a volcano with a desert and jungle secondary island. The islands were just spheres I created with WorldEdit. I stored all the game logic inside the spheres. The best part about the map was the timer system; I had linked hundreds of repeaters to a command block that teleported all the players inside the volcano for a final showdown a couple minutes after game start. The server and world save file were on my Macbook that was stolen by the interior painters that came into my dorm when I was away during summer break.

My personal favorite part of that map was the broken staircase winding down the side of the main island sphere. There were fence posts and cobwebs, so if you weren't careful in your movements, you would get stuck. It was carved into the side of the stone, so if you made a wrong move, you could trip and fall off. This is where many intense battles happened; after players lost a fight and were on the run, they would retreat to the bottom of the stairs or start climbing the great jungle tree. Since the fall into the void is always fatal, even high health players in pursuit of weak prey must take their time to descend. Since climbing speed is static, if a player got to the tree before their opponent could get within striking range, they could regenerate a heart or two before the climb was finished.

When I played Elder Scrolls Online, the only way a solo player could survive a zerg of PvPers stampeding through was to use the environment to break line of sight to divide and conquer. I devised builds to play around this strategy, such as a stamina sorcerer that used the lightning dash for mobility, and set traps for those who follow. The best build for the "zerg surfing", made by JackDaniell or KristoferESO, was a templar vampire that combined the vampire elusive mist with the bow roll dodge movement speed boost. Using the mobility and semi-stealth, a player could quickly reposition into the rocks or down a cliff, even with 10 soldiers shooting poisoned arrows. The mist form would reduce the damage received, and then the templar cleansing ritual would remove all the negative effects accumulated from tanking so many players. Whatever players followed the vampire mist apparition to maintain line of sight would be surprised when Jack broke the mist spell early to fire an arrow and weave in a javelin of light, knocking them back. The bow would be sheathed and the greatsword drawn; the knockback synergized with the greatsword critical charge, which increased damage based on distance traveled. The typical zergling, emboldened by numbers and frenzied in his love towards Khorne, would have likely forgotten to refresh his defensive combat buff rotation. If he were to die, in some crevice in the rocks, his zerg would find him later, his body drained of health and stamina by the templar "repentance" ritual. They would pump in a charged grand soul gem, and he would get back up and do it all over again.

Sun Tzu said that an ideal condition for fighting is with the high ground, while the sun shines on your back. This way, your enemies will be looking up at it through you and be blinded.

Skyrim's Shriekwind Bastion features a compelling level feature wherein the player enters at the base of the mountain, and in progressing through the level reaches a lookout point at the mountain's top that also serves as an exit, so long as the player has the "slow fall" enchantment on their boots.

In Dark Souls 3, while approaching the Profaned Capitol, the caves give way to a quick cliff's edge, with a romantic view of the mountains in the distance. Here the player can use their dragon form to access the Archdragon Peak.

In Sol Eremus, I want to give players the sense that you get when conquering Shriekwind Bastion, or see Archdragon Peak after the Irithyll Dungeon. You enter into the caves in some place, and you are lost in the dark. Just when you give up hope, following a tunnel even further down, you are exposed to some rubble and a little temple or staging area of sorts. There is an outlook, where the sunlight is streaming in. You can finally get an idea of how far you have come, via looking down at the place you started.
